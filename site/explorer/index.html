Seed stability

8 seeds of one GBDT configuration on HI-Medium, changing nothing but the seed. The bars below are each metric’s observed range expressed as a percentage of its own mean, which is the only form in which metrics on different scales can be compared at all.

What this does and does not say

It says that a single-seed headline from this configuration could have landed anywhere in the printed range. It does not separate seed sensitivity from the window’s own thin-day structure, and it says nothing about any other rung: HI-Large has three seeds, not eight, because each fit took about 26 minutes and 30 of 31 GB on a quota that could not be raised.

Why a budget changes what a metric means

A single-day illustration with made-up inputs

Nothing in this simulator reads the project’s data. It demonstrates five identities on one day. The project’s own evaluation is slot-weighted across days of very different size, so feeding totals from a whole window in here does not reproduce any published number — that pooling error is precisely what the harness exists to expose.

The five identities
  • Prevalence is P / N.
  • Random-ranker precision@k equals prevalence: a uniform ranker fills its k slots with the base rate.
  • Random-ranker recall@k is min(k, N) / N, the share of the population it manages to review.
  • The attainable recall ceiling is min(P, k) / P: no ranker can recall what it has no slot for.
  • The attainable precision ceiling is min(P, k) / min(k, N): a perfect ranker still has to fill every slot it is given.
